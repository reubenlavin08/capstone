## Q&A — likely prompts (assessor guide lists these)

The context guide tells assessors to ask these. Have natural answers ready, not memorized:

**"What surprised you most?"**
→ How much more you learned from failure than from success. The shorted IMU and the wrong-layer compressed-prompt story are the two you'll remember.

**"If you had another month, what would you do with it?"**
→ Pre-arrange three formal expert interviews with prep'd questions. Tune the ToF sensor on the helmet so the demo would have cleaner data.

**"Something you wanted to explore but didn't?"**
→ Training a model from scratch. Everything you shipped used existing AI tools. That's why USGS seismic is the next probe.

**"One thing you wish you'd known at the start?"**
→ That credentials and artifacts are different categories of evidence — and the "developer" rubric is the second one. Would have started shipping in March, not April.

**"What was the vibe-coded custom AI solution for the Pro Cert?"**
→ Honest answer: "The Pro Cert description references a vibe-coded solution. I demonstrated that competency across the broader GitHub portfolio rather than a single dedicated project — most of those 16 repos were built with Claude Code as a coding collaborator."

## Delivery cues (re-read Monday morning)

- **Look at the assessor**, not the slides. The slides are for them, not you.
- **Pause after each named lesson** — count 1, 2 in your head. Silence makes it land.
- **Don't read bullets aloud.** If you say what's on screen, the slide is doing your job.
- **Match the room** — 1-on-1 conversation volume, not stage. Sit-forward energy.
- **Strongest material at start and end** — first 2 min and last 2 min are what they remember.
- **"What I'd do differently"** beats "what I built." Lean on reconstruction over reporting.
